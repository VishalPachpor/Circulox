Choosing a jurisdiction: start with the business

The information to organise before discussing where to establish your company.

Describe the activity before choosing the location

Start with what the business will do, who its customers will be and where it expects to operate. A jurisdiction discussion is more useful when it begins with an operating picture rather than a list of countries.

Make ownership and decision-making visible

Prepare an outline of the founders, shareholders and any existing companies in the group. Include where the people making business decisions are based. This helps frame the questions for your adviser.

Bring banking and investment plans into the discussion

Describe the currencies, payment flows and banking arrangements the business expects to need. If fundraising is planned, include the proposed timing, investor requirements and any existing documents.

Prepare a short brief

A useful first brief includes the proposed activities, operating markets, ownership outline and your main questions. The appropriate structure and engagement scope can then be discussed around those facts.
