This is a writing brief, not a redesign or approved legal advice. Proposed additions are requests for content, not features or services already confirmed.

1. What this page needs to do

A founder arrives, recognises their legal need, sees credible evidence and knows how to arrange a relevant call.

Preserve the approved editorial hierarchy. Make service fit, actual expertise and the next step clear within the opening screen.

Length guidance: Approximately 450–650 words across the whole page; respect the short budgets for individual homepage sections.

2. Content to develop, section by section

1. Opening

One plain-language H1, a 35–50 word introduction and the existing primary/secondary actions. Explain the firm and who it serves before asking for a call.

2. Selected clients

Keep the approved moving logos. Supply any desired heading and one restrained context sentence; no invented engagement details or testimonials.

3. The firm

A 60–90 word statement of the firm's actual approach and scope. Replace generic claims with one or two owner-confirmed distinctions.

4. Our expertise

Keep the four current groups and all eight service links. Supply a 20–30 word explanation for each group, using the visitor's problem rather than internal legal jargon.

5. Jurisdiction advisory

Explain the decision factors in 35–50 words. Review all eight country summaries and their three discussion topics. Link to the country pages, not another booking action.

6. Insights & guides

Provide one short editorial introduction. Only published, reviewed articles appear; do not invent article cards to fill the layout.

7. From X

Keep the three owner-selected native posts and the @iptishax profile link. Suggest a short section introduction only if useful; do not rewrite tweets as the firm's claims.

8. Consultation

One final booking action, a 30–45 word description of what to share and any owner-confirmed expectation about the call.

3. Questions for the owner

  • What two or three verifiable aspects of the firm's approach distinguish it?
  • Are any client case summaries separately approved for public use?
  • Who normally handles the first call, and what can be accurately said about its purpose?

Record answers in a separate document or paste them into the prompt below. This HTML is a portable reference file; it does not save edits.

Write for Circulox, an international legal and business advisory firm serving startups, fintech, Web3 and established enterprises. The business goal is a relevant consultation, reached through clear information and credible evidence. Use calm, specific English, short paragraphs and useful headings. Keep the premium editorial tone; avoid hype, generic slogans, emojis, repeated contact buttons and keyword stuffing.
Separate supplied facts from proposed wording. Existing website copy is a draft, not independent proof of a legal rule or an achievement. Never invent offices, admissions, team qualifications, client engagements, case results, testimonials, success rates, years in business, partnerships, turnaround times, prices, tax benefits or guaranteed approvals. Client logos are approved for display; they do not establish the scope or outcome of any engagement. Do not generate endorsements from that list.
For current laws, filing requirements, licensing, tax, costs or deadlines, use dated primary official sources and identify the relevant jurisdiction. If browsing or confirmation is unavailable, use [LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED: question] instead of guessing. Separate legal requirements from Circulox's own process. Do not imply that incorporation authorises regulated activity or guarantees a bank account. Flag material claims for the firm's legal review.
Use only one primary booking destination: https://calendly.com/iptisha. Email alternative: iptisha@circulox.org. Do not call the consultation free or promise a response time without approval. Do not create login, editor or admin navigation in public copy. Keep substantive detail useful on desktop, with a concise summary first for phone readers. Put longer secondary material in FAQs or future guides; do not turn every section into a list.
